Educational Tokenizer vs Production LLMs

This playground demonstrates deterministic word/punctuation tokenization. Modern production LLMs (like GPT-4, Gemini, and Llama) use Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) or WordPiece, which recursively merges frequent subword pairs. A word like "unbreakable" might become ["un", "break", "able"]. This prevents the model from ever failing on unknown or misspelled words!

📐 Understanding 3D Projections (PCA & Dimensionality Reduction)

In real-world language models, embedding vectors live in spaces with 768 to 12,288 dimensions! Since human eyes cannot perceive higher than 3 dimensions, scientists use Principal Component Analysis (PCA) or t-SNE / UMAP to project high-dimensional variance onto 3 orthogonal axes $[X, Y, Z]$.

Educational Notice: The 3D coordinates shown here are illustrative reduced-dimensional projections, not the raw 16-D or 4,096-D vectors. The glowing dashed line visualizes the trajectory of your input prompt moving through semantic space before Transformer attention contextualizes each word!

⚠️ Essential AI Distinction

Educational Notice

Important distinction: Adding a word here adds it to this application's educational vocabulary dictionary and calculates an initial embedding vector for visualization.

In a real pre-trained LLM (such as GPT-4, Llama 3, or Claude), the vocabulary is strictly fixed during pre-training. Adding a new word does NOT change the weights, parameters, or internal layers of the neural network.

To support new words in production, engineers either break them into known subwords (via Byte-Pair Encoding) or perform full continual pre-training and fine-tuning with expanded embedding matrices.

Demystifying LLM Pipelines

Clear, beginner-friendly answers to the most fundamental questions about how language models convert human thoughts into mathematics.

1. What is a Token?

A token is the atomic unit of text that an LLM reads and generates. It can be an entire word, part of a word (a subword or syllable), a punctuation symbol, or even a single whitespace.

"apple" ➔ 1 token | "unbelievable" ➔ ["un", "believ", "able"] (3 tokens)

2. Why Do LLMs Tokenize Text?

Computers cannot natively read sentences. Neural networks are composed of mathematical matrix multiplications that require discrete numerical indices. Tokenization maps infinite raw text combinations into a manageable, discrete set of IDs.

Human string ➔ Discrete Tokens ➔ Fixed-size Math Input

3. What is a Vocabulary?

The vocabulary is the model's fixed master dictionary. Every token the model can ever see or speak is indexed in this table. Most modern models maintain vocabularies between 32,000 and 256,000 unique tokens.

Vocabulary = { 0: "<pad>", 1: "<unk>", ..., 57: "king", ... }

4. What is a Token ID?

A Token ID is the unique integer assigned to each entry in the vocabulary. It serves as an array index that points to the exact row in the model's weight matrix.

"attention" ➔ Token ID #32 ➔ row 32 of Embedding Table

5. What are Embeddings?

Embeddings are dense numerical representations of tokens. Instead of arbitrary integers, each word is mapped to a vector of floating-point numbers where semantic concepts are encoded geometrically.

"queen" ➔ [0.30, -0.70, 0.60, -0.50, 0.90, ...]

6. Why Vectors Instead of Numbers?

A single number cannot capture the richness of human language. A vector with dozens or thousands of dimensions can simultaneously encode multiple independent properties: tense, gender, sentiment, part of speech, and physical size.

Dim 1: living vs nonliving | Dim 2: royal vs common | Dim 3: scale

7. What is 3D Projection?

Because human minds cannot perceive 16 or 4,096 dimensions, mathematical algorithms like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) find the three axes of greatest statistical variance and plot the tokens in 3D $[X, Y, Z]$ space.

16-Dimensional Point ➔ Matrix Projection ➔ 3D Coordinates

8. Why Do Related Words Cluster?

During training, the model adjusts vector weights so words that frequently appear in similar textual contexts (like "dog" and "wolf", or "neural" and "transformer") end up pointing in almost the same direction in vector space.

Cosine Similarity("king", "queen") > Cosine Similarity("king", "apple")
